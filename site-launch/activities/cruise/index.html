Fort Lauderdale River Cruise

Also recognized as the Las Olas River cruise (named after the iconic street that runs parallel to the river), our standard route takes you through the renowned New River. The New River got its name early in US history when settlers were still mapping the area. The mouth of the river would constantly change, leading surveyors to mark it as a “New River.” Or so the story goes, at least. What isn’t up for debate is that the New River cuts right through the heart of downtown, acts as a vital transportation waterway, and is the perfect location for day cruises in Fort Lauderdale.

A New River cruise is the perfect place for anyone who wants to take in the sites of multi-million dollar yachts and houses. As the river approaches downtown, luxury homes disappear while restaurants and other attractions take their place. A collection of restaurants and bars occupy the sides and is visited by tourists and locals alike. The Fort Lauderdale Riverwalk borders part of the river and is a hotbed for local events and shops. Parks dot the sides of the river and break up the urban sprawl to provide plenty of visual variety.

Explore

Explore the river’s edges while on your yacht cruise in Fort Lauderdale. You will see a few famous landmarks as you go down the river. A favorite amongst many is the Stranahan House. Initially built as a trading outpost over 100 years ago, this is the oldest standing building in Broward County. Next to it, you’ll find the Riverside Hotel. Opened in 1936, the hotel has been a landmark of Las Olas Blvd since its inception. Its Mediterranean style would serve to influence many of the buildings that followed.

Sightsee

Along with a rich history, our Fort Lauderdale boat cruises put on display a level of opulence that is hard to come by in other parts of the country. As you pass downtown, you’ll reach the beginning of what is colloquially known as the Millionaires’ Row boat tour. Owners of these mega-mansions range from lawyers, business owners, executives of billion-dollar companies, lucky children, and everything in between. With each house displaying the tastes of their wealthy owners, uninhibited by finances, there’s a great collection of properties to see.

Adventure

While the New River terminates near the sandbar, the Intracoastal Waterway extends up and down the coast, separated only by South Florida’s barrier islands. If you’d like to continue a calm boat cruise, this waterway is another great candidate to take in the sights and sounds of Florida. Alternatively, you can venture through the nearby Port Everglades and continue your adventures into the Atlantic Ocean.

Dine

As boating is so prevalent in the area, it’s unsurprising that waterfront restaurants cater to boaters. Taking advantage of their waterfront locations, a handful of restaurants offer dockage, allowing your Fort Lauderdale river cruise to transform into a dinner cruise. If you’re unsure of what’s in the area, don’t hesitate to ask your captain for recommendations so that you can have the perfect meal.
